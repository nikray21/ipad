## 01 · What changed — The $25,000 gate is gone

**On screen:** Since June 4, 2026, day trades are no longer counted.  
**Target:** 15s

On June 4, 2026 FINRA's amended Rule 4210 took effect and the pattern-day-trader framework was deleted outright — the designation, the day-trade count, the day-trading buying-power formula and the $25,000 minimum equity requirement that had stood since 2001. The SEC approved the amendments on April 14, 2026 (Securities Exchange Act Release No. 105226); FINRA published Regulatory Notice 26-10. Brokers moved fast: Webull, Robinhood, Fidelity and tastytrade were live day one, Schwab on June 8, E*TRADE on June 9 — though firms have until October 20, 2027 to finish implementing the full framework, so a smaller broker may still be running the old rules.

---

## 02 · The fine print — Three floors are still standing

**On screen:** One gate opened. Three others nobody mentioned stayed shut.  
**Target:** 15s

This is the part the headlines skipped. The repeal removed one specific gate and left three things in place that bind a beginner much harder: (1) the $2,000 minimum equity to open a margin account at all, which was never part of the repeal; (2) the cash-account regime you land in below that number, where 3 good-faith violations in a rolling twelve months restricts you to settled cash for 90 days; and (3) the new intraday-margin standard's own penalty — repeatedly failing to satisfy an intraday margin deficit within 5 business days freezes the account for 90 days. The 90-day punishment did not leave a beginner's life. It just changed which rule delivers it.

---

## 03 · The real floor — The floor moved. It did not vanish.

**On screen:** Margin accounts still start at $2,000 — that never changed.  
**Target:** 15s

FINRA Rule 4210's $2,000 minimum equity requirement to open and maintain a margin account is a separate provision from the pattern-day-trader rule, and the June 2026 amendments left it completely untouched — FINRA's own interpretations of Rule 4210, valid from June 4, 2026, still state it. So the honest headline is not "the floor is gone", it is "the floor dropped from $25,000 to $2,000". That is a real and large change. It is not the same as no floor.

---

## 04 · Below the minimum — Under that, you are in a cash account

**On screen:** Every dollar has to settle overnight before you can use it again.  
**Target:** 15s

If you start with less than $2,000 you are not in a margin account, you are in a cash account — and cash accounts run on settlement. Stocks, ETFs and options settle one business day after the trade (T+1, since the industry moved off T+2 in May 2024). Sell on Monday and the proceeds are usable Tuesday. Buy something with those proceeds before they settle, then sell that position before settlement lands, and you have committed a good-faith violation. Nothing in the pattern-day-trader repeal touched any of this.

---

## 05 · The other 90 days — Three strikes and you are frozen

**On screen:** 3 good-faith violations in a year — then 90 days of settled cash only.  
**Target:** 15s

Most brokers treat the first two good-faith violations as warnings. The third inside a rolling twelve-month window restricts the account to settled-cash status for 90 calendar days, meaning you must have fully settled cash before opening any position. That is the same 90-day length as the old pattern-day-trader restriction everybody celebrated losing — it is simply a different rule, in a different account type, aimed at the exact traders who start small. Sources: Fidelity, Schwab, E*TRADE and tastytrade all publish the same mechanics.

## 06 · What did not change — We already know what no gate looks like

**On screen:** That rule never applied there. 97% still lost money.  
**Target:** 15s

The pattern-day-trader rule was a FINRA rule, so it governed US brokerage accounts and nowhere else. Brazil is the cleanest natural experiment we have: Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti tracked everyone who began day trading Brazilian equity futures between 2013 and 2015 and found that of those who persisted more than 300 days, 97% lost money — only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage. Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean's fifteen-year Taiwan study found fewer than 1% of day traders were predictably profitable net of fees. Removing a capital gate does not change any of that. It only changes who is allowed to find out.

## 07 · The real question — The regulator never set your number

**On screen:** Your stop loss sets your minimum — not FINRA.  
**Target:** 15s

The $25,000 figure existed to manage the broker's credit risk on intraday margin lending. It was never an estimate of what a person needs to trade competently, and treating its removal as permission is the mistake this whole video exists to prevent. The number that actually binds you is arithmetic: how much you are willing to lose on one trade, divided by how far away your stop sits. That ratio decides the size of every position you are allowed to hold, and it does not care what the rulebook says.

## 08 · The math — Two percent risk, one honest formula

**On screen:** Risk ÷ stop distance = position size. Nothing else.  
**Target:** 15s

Risk 2% of the account on any one trade — the same rule this channel uses everywhere. If the stop sits 5% below your entry, then the position you are allowed to hold is 2 ÷ 5 = 40% of the account. On $5,000 that means risking $100, holding $2,000 of stock, and a target at twice the risk paying $200. Every figure on this slide is computed from those two percentages — change either one and the whole deck updates.

## 09 · Run the numbers — The same math, four account sizes

**On screen:** At $500 a good win pays $20. That is the problem.  
**Target:** 15s

The formula does not break at small size — it just stops being worth doing. At $500 you risk $10 to make $20, and the temptation is to fix that by risking far more than 2%, which is exactly how small accounts die. At $5,000 the same discipline risks $100 to make $200 — small enough to survive a losing streak, large enough that following the rule feels worth it. That second condition is not greed, it is what keeps you obeying the system on trade number thirty.

---

## 10 · Where it breaks — Small accounts cannot size precisely

**On screen:** One share is all or nothing — no risk system survives that.  
**Target:** 15s

Here is the mechanical reason the tiny account fails, and it has nothing to do with regulation. Shares come in whole numbers. On a $180 stock, a $500 account can hold exactly 1 share — so your only position sizes are zero and one, and the risk you actually take is whatever that single share happens to imply. At $5,000 you hold 11 shares and can size to within about 9%. Fractional shares help, but order-type support for them varies by broker — several restrict fractional orders to plain market orders, which breaks the bracket-order workflow the system depends on. Check yours before you rely on it.

## 11 · My answer — What I would actually start with

**On screen:** $2,000 opens a margin account. $5,000 makes the system work.  
**Target:** 15s

So here is the number, and it is not the one either headline gives you. $2,000 is the legal floor — below it you are in a cash account, dodging settlement violations, and the system's bracket orders get awkward. $5,000 is where the arithmetic actually starts working: $100 of risk per trade, 11 shares of a $180 stock, roughly 9% sizing precision, and a 2R win that pays $200. That is my opinion, not a rule — and it is money you have already decided you can lose without changing anything about your life.

## 12 · The first 90 days — What I would do with it

**On screen:** One position at a time, until twenty trades are logged.  
**Target:** 15s

Whatever you start with, the first ninety days are for evidence, not profit. One position at a time so you are learning the process rather than managing a book. Log every trade — date, setup, plan, result, one honest note — because the Brazil data found no evidence that screen time alone teaches anyone anything; the review is where the learning happens. Twenty trades before you judge the system, because four wins in a row is noise at this sample size. And never top the account up to erase a drawdown: that converts a bounded loss into an unbounded one.

---

## 13 · What I would not do — The four ways this money disappears

**On screen:** Every one of these ignores the stop you already set.  
**Target:** 15s

Borrowing on margin to feel bigger multiplies a position you could not size properly in the first place. Skipping the stop does not remove the risk, it removes your ability to measure it — without a stop there is no position size, only a guess. Trading the open because the gate is gone ignores that the spread and the volatility at 9:30 never went anywhere. And adding to a loser is the single fastest way a small account becomes no account. Each one breaks a rule the system already gave you.
