# Copywriting — words are design

Layout can only carry copy that's already good. Vague, hedged, or bloated words
make even a beautiful composition feel hollow. Treat every string as a design
decision.

## Principles

- **Specific beats generic.** "Deploys in 40ms" > "Blazing fast." "12,000
  teams" > "Trusted by many." Concrete detail is credibility.
- **Lead with the benefit, back it with the mechanism.** What the reader gets,
  then how it works — not the reverse.
- **One idea per line.** If a headline has a comma-splice of two ideas, split
  or cut.
- **Verbs > nouns.** "Ship faster" beats "Acceleration of shipping." Nominalized
  verbs ("utilization", "implementation") are where energy goes to die.
- **Cut hedges & filler.** Delete: very, really, just, actually, simply,
  basically, in order to, leverage, empower, seamless, robust, solutions,
  cutting-edge, best-in-class, revolutionary, game-changing.
- **Sentence case** usually reads more human and modern than Title Case for
  headings and buttons.
- **Active voice.** "We reduced load time" not "Load time was reduced."
- **Read it aloud.** If you stumble or run out of breath, rewrite.

## By element

- **Hero headline:** one clear promise, 3–9 words. Not a tagline pun that says
  nothing. The subhead earns the click with the specific how.
- **CTAs:** verb + value. "Start free trial", "See it in action", "Get the
  template" — never "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" (learn more about
  *what*?).
- **Section headers:** state the conclusion, not the topic. "Revenue doubled
  after launch" > "Results."
- **Feature copy:** name → benefit in plain language → (optional) the how. Skip
  the icon-plus-two-words-that-mean-nothing pattern.
- **Empty states:** encouraging + instructive. Tell the user what this is and
  the one action to start.
- **Errors:** what happened, why (if useful), and how to fix it — in human
  words, no codes-only.
- **Microcopy:** labels, tooltips, placeholders, confirmations. This is where
  products feel considerate. Placeholders show format, they don't replace
  labels.
- **Charts/slides:** title = the takeaway, not the axis. "Support tickets fell
  60%", not "Ticket volume by month."

## Voice & tone

- Pick a voice on two axes: formal↔casual and serious↔playful. Hold it
  everywhere — a playful hero with a corporate footer feels fake.
- Match tone to moment: confident in marketing, calm and clear in errors,
  celebratory (briefly) in success.
- Write to one reader as "you." Avoid "users."

## Anti-slop

- No stat slop (numbers that impress no one and mean nothing).
- No fake testimonials, fake logos, invented data. If you don't have it, don't
  fabricate it — leave the section out or mark it as placeholder for the user.
- No thesaurus flexing. Plain words, well arranged, win.
- Don't pad to fill a layout. If a section has nothing to say, cut the section.
